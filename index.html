Income & Education in Denmark

A comprehensive analysis of socioeconomic factors, industry, and wealth across municipalities.

Introduction

Welcome to our final project for the DTU Social Data course. In this data story, we analyze the intricate relationships between average income, education levels, and employment rates across different municipalities in Denmark using data from Statistics Denmark (HFUDD16 and IFOR35).

We aim to uncover how geographical location dictates wealth, which industries act as the biggest financial drivers, and how education pipelines feed into these economic ecosystems.

Part 1: The Geographical Distribution of Income & Demographics

The National Trend

From 2008 to 2024, average incomes across Danish municipalities have shown a steady, consistent upward trajectory. This general increase aligns closely with standard economic inflation over the 16-year period. Rather than localized, explosive economic booms, the data establishes a stable baseline of wealth growth nationwide.

Demographic Uniformity

Despite the differences in overall wealth, the fundamental socioeconomic makeup of these municipalities remains surprisingly uniform. The ratios of employed, unemployed, and enrolled individuals show only slight variations geographically. This suggests that regional income disparities are largely driven by the type of industries present, rather than the sheer volume of the available workforce.

The Vejen & Billund Anomalies

While national trends are steady, dramatic localized shocks do occur. Between 2022 and 2023, average incomes spiked massively in both Vejen (from 314k to 446k DKK) and Billund (from 329k to 470k DKK). Interestingly, their paths diverged: Billund's income corrected back down to 340k DKK the following year, whereas Vejen maintained its newfound wealth plateau, stabilizing at 435k DKK.
