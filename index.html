Income & Education in Denmark

A comprehensive analysis of socioeconomic factors, industry, and wealth across municipalities.

Introduction

Welcome to our final project for the DTU Social Data course. In this data story, we analyze the intricate relationships between average income, education levels, and employment rates across different municipalities in Denmark using data from Statistics Denmark (HFUDD16 and IFOR35).

We aim to uncover how geographical location dictates wealth, which industries act as the biggest financial drivers, and how education pipelines feed into these economic ecosystems.

Part 1: The Geographical Distribution of Income & Demographics

National trend

From 2008 to 2024, average incomes across Danish municipalities have shown a steady, consistent upward trajectory. This general increase aligns closely with standard economic inflation over the 16-year period. Rather than localized, explosive economic booms, the data establishes a stable baseline of wealth growth nationwide.

Demographic Uniformity

Despite differences in overall wealth, municipalities share a consistent socioeconomic profile. The proportions of employed, unemployed, and enrolled individuals changes only slightly across the country, as you can see in the ternary plot below where all the data points lie on a thin strip of the chart space. This suggests that average municipal income is largely independent of the size of the workforce, but rather dependent on the types of local industries.

The Vejen & Billund Anomalies

While national trends are steady, average incomes in Vejen and Billund experienced an anomalous spike between 2022 and 2023, jumping from 314k to 446k DKK and from 329k to 470k DKK, respectively. Following this surge, however, their trajectories diverged: Billund's average income corrected downward to 340k DKK the following year, while Vejen's remained elevated, stabilizing at 435k DKK.

Part 3: How Education Shapes Denmark’s Labour Market

A More Educated Denmark

Across Denmark, the data shows a clear shift toward higher educational attainment over time. A larger share of the population get a master’s degree and PhD's. At the same time, industries such as Consultancy, R&D and Business Services grow, while Education, Health, Arts and Recreation remain major sectors. This suggests that a more highly educated population is increasingly linked to knowledge-intensive and public-service industries.

Lyngby-Taarbæk and Kolding Follow Different Paths

Comparing Lyngby-Taarbæk and Kolding highlights two different municipal profiles. Lyngby-Taarbæk has a higher share of people with master’s and PhD degrees, reflecting its strong connection to education, research and consultancy. Kolding has a larger share of people with bachelor-level or lower education across many industries, pointing to a more industrial and business-oriented structure. Still, both municipalities show a rise in master’s degree holders over time.

Education Helps Explain Industry and Employment

Kolding has a higher employed share across much of the period, but employment also fluctuates more, especially after the financial crisis in 2008, likely because manufacturing and production-related industries play a larger role. Lyngby-Taarbæk shows a more stable pattern, probably because larger proportion of population are linked to education, research and knowledge-intensive industries. Together, the comparison shows how education levels help shape local industry structure and labour-market patterns across Denmark.
